Artwork and caption

The first flowers can be setting fruit while later flowers are still opening. Nutrition timing needs to account for both.

Timing follows the block. September–October is the planning window, not a fixed flowering date. Cultivar, region and seasonal conditions change the timing. Use the stage observed in each block to decide when this post is relevant.

Draft caption

The first flowers don’t wait for the rest of the block.

A cherry block can still look full of blossom while some of its earliest flowers have already set fruit. That spread matters when you’re timing nutrition.

The tree is supporting flowers and early fruit with stored reserves and a changing supply from its roots and leaves. Waiting for the whole block to look ready can overlook what the earliest flowers already need.

When you review your bud and flower program, look at how far the first flowers have progressed as well as what is still opening. Your Hybrid Ag agronomist can help match the next nutrition decision to that progression.

Source notes

The flowering story is supported by cherry research and informed by Hybrid Ag’s internal agronomy and marketing discussions.

  • Hort Innovation: Understanding flowering, fruit set and crop load in cherries (CY12003). Cherry flowering and fruit-set research supports treating flowering as a progression. The artwork illustrates overlapping stages; it is not a timeline or a prediction that every flower will set fruit.
  • Cherry reproductive-development research, Annals of Botany. Primary research on sweet cherry flower and early fruit development. No universal day count or fixed cell-division window is used in this concept.
  • Australian Cherry Production Guide, pp. 73–76. Pollination, environmental conditions and crop development provide the wider block and cultivar context.
  • Internal source context: Emily Corbett’s TALES reproductive-phases talk, Hamilton Rowe’s TALES cell-expansion talk, the August 2020 crop-load webinar, and the September 2025 marketing discussion with Andrew Smith and Hamilton Rowe. The crop-load webinar uses an orchard photo to explain early flowers progressing before the block reaches full bloom. Historical rates and absolute timing claims have not been carried into this draft.
  • Hybrid Ag Bud & Flower review package, July 2026, and Cherry Nutrition Playbook, p. 42 (internal source). The planned nutrition and DSA sequence includes flowering checks before shuck fall. This post addresses flowering progression rather than replacing the block’s program.

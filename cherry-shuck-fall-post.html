Cherry Shuck fall

Shuck fall · September 2026 planning window
A program DSA checkpoint

Spring 2026 · Hybrid Ag

Artwork and caption

A post about getting the sample, the result and the nutrition decision into the same growing window.

Timing follows the block. September is the calendar prompt. Shuck fall follows the block’s flowering and fruit development, so the observed stage sets the sampling timing. It is a program checkpoint; flowering checks may already have taken place.

Draft caption

Your cherries keep developing while the sample is at the lab.

At shuck fall, cell division in young fruit is helping to establish their size potential. A sap result is most useful when there is still time to act on what it tells us.

That means planning collection, dispatch and the discussion of the results around the block’s progress. We read the paired DSA results alongside earlier tests, recent inputs and the stage the trees have reached before deciding whether the nutrition program needs to change.

If your block is nearing shuck fall, speak with your Hybrid Ag agronomist about the sample and when you’ll review the results together.

Source notes

Relevant production, sampling and Playbook sections checked for this draft.

  • Australian Cherry Production Guide. Growth and development, p. 76. Supports the distinction between early cell division and subsequent fruit growth; this concept makes no fixed-day, fruit-size or yield promise.
  • NovaCropControl sweet cherry sampling guide, pp. 2–3. Supports paired sampling of the youngest fully developed and oldest still-vital leaves. Use the current Hybrid Ag sampling kit instructions for collection, packing and dispatch.
  • Hybrid Ag Technical & Sales Meeting, 28 August 2026 (internal discussion). The team discussed results arriving in time for a nutrition decision, and the trade-off between turnaround and consistency of historical test data. This concept makes no laboratory turnaround promise.
  • Hybrid Ag Cherry Nutrition Playbook, p. 42 (internal source). The final Cherry Nutrition Playbook, p. 42, includes flowering and shuck-fall DSA checks. Shuck fall is described here as a checkpoint, not the first sample of the season.

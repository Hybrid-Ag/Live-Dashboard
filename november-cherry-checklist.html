Cherry post-harvest checklist

Before the last pick, have a plan for what comes next. Use this checklist with your agronomist to review the block, decide whether a DSA would be useful and work out what needs attention after harvest.

01

Review the block

  • Note the variety, block and harvest dates. If picking is still ahead, put a time aside to review the block once it is finished.
  • Bring the season’s yield, pack-out and fruit-quality records where available. Note anything that differed across the block, including crop load or uneven growth.
  • Gather recent soil, leaf, sap or DSA results, along with the Nutrients applied and any problems during the season. Existing results are worth discussing.
02

Check the canopy and water supply

  • Look at how much healthy, active leaf remains. Note early leaf loss, stress or uneven areas rather than assuming every tree needs the same treatment.
  • Check irrigation performance, soil moisture and any known root or water-quality limitations. Nutrition decisions need that context.
  • Discuss what the trees need while leaves are still active. The aim is to support their condition after harvest without encouraging unnecessary late growth.
03

Make DSA part of the conversation

  • Ask whether paired young and older leaf samples would help answer a specific question in this block. Confirm the crop stage and suitable leaf material before sampling.
  • Follow the laboratory’s current collection and transport instructions. Record the block, variety, sample positions, date, time and recent applications.
  • Review the two results together with field observations and other tests. If repeating a DSA, use comparable sampling conditions so the comparison is useful.

A plan for your block

04

Turn the review into a decision

  • Identify the question the testing needs to answer. A new test may be useful, or existing results may already provide a starting point.
  • Where the evidence supports a Nutrient requirement, ask about a Hybrid-Ag prescription blend suited to the block, application equipment and budget. Avoid adding Nutrients simply because harvest has finished.
  • Write down the agreed action and when to review it. That may mean a targeted application, further investigation or no change yet.
A DSA compares Nutrient concentrations in sap from different leaf ages. It does not directly measure the tree’s stored carbohydrates or whole-tree reserves, and it does not prove how much Nutrient reached the fruit.

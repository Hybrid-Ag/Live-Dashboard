02 · What has to happen inside the flower
Cherries · Spring 2026Grower question 02

Before you blame the bees, check four things.

In Andrew's words. A light set is usually more than one of these.
1

Weather through flowering

Frost, a cold snap or days of rain slow the tube down. It has a short time to reach the ovule.

2

Pollinisers and bees

Did the polliniser rows overlap your main variety, and were the bees flying on the days that counted?

3

Water and roots

A dry or waterlogged root zone through flowering cuts what the tree can take up, Boron included. Supply in the soil is not the same as access.

4

What went in

Boron, Calcium and Zinc in the leaf or sap results before flowering, read against the soil test. If they were short going in, the flowers were working with less.

When the results show a trace was short, we build a prescription blend for that block ahead of next flowering, with Opti-Trace Boron where a foliar Boron fits.

01 · The question
Horticulture · Spring 2026Grower question 03

Count it. Ten limbs, ten minutes.

A number beats an impression, and it travels to the next decision.
1

Tag ten fruiting limbs across the block. Not the easy row, not the best tree. Flag tape and a marker.

2

At full flowering, count the flowers on each limb and write it on the tag. Missed it this year? Start at step 3 and carry the fruit count to harvest.

3

After shuck fall (cherries) or the drop (apples, stone fruit), count the fruit that held.

4

Fruit held ÷ flowers counted = your set. Average the ten. That is your crop load, in your block, this season.

02 · The method
Horticulture · Spring 2026Grower question 03

The number changes the next decision.

Andrew on what he looks at next, depending on where the count lands.

Light

Well under what the flowering promised

The question goes back to flowering: weather, pollination, water, and what the leaf results said was in the tree. Ask it now while it's fresh, so the lead-in to next flowering changes.

About right

Load the trees can carry

Protect it. Water to the load, keep the canopy working, and keep Calcium arriving while the cells are being laid down. A paired sap test tells you whether it is.

Heavy

More fruit than the tree can finish

Every fruit shares the same Calcium and sugar supply. Size and firmness are what's at stake. Crop load, water and Calcium delivery are the levers left, read off the test, not the calendar.

Whichever column you're in, the next step is the same: measure first, understand second, intervene third. Where the results call for it, we build a prescription blend for that block.

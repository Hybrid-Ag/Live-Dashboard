DSA, standard leaf testing and seasonal trends

Differential Sap Analysis (also called a Differential Leaf Extract Test) compares Nutrients in fluid extracted separately from young and older leaves collected together from the same block.

What the paired results add

Separate young and older leaf results help your agronomist investigate Nutrient concerns, prioritise follow-up and assess whether a prescription or its timing needs changing. The results are read with soil, water, crop growth and recent applications.

ComparisonStandard leaf tissue testDSA / paired-leaf sap analysis
SampleThe crop-specific leaf or plant part required by the laboratory.Young and older leaves collected together and kept as separately identified samples.
MeasurementNutrient concentrations in dried plant material, reported on a dry-matter basis.Nutrient concentrations in extracted leaf fluid, reported separately for each leaf age. The panel may also include sugars, pH and EC.
Useful forAssessing the sampled tissue against a suitable crop-stage reference and following consistent repeat samples.Comparing the two leaf ages at a crop stage, then following changes across comparable repeat pairs.

The methods can complement one another. Sampling protocols, units and reference values differ, so the numbers are not directly interchangeable. Neither test establishes the cause of a result on its own.

Build a seasonal record

A series of comparable DSAs at relevant crop stages builds a record of how the young and older leaf results change through the season. Read alongside applications, weather, crop load and harvest or quality records, it can help identify recurring Nutrient concerns and refine the following season’s timing and prescriptions.

Keep the crop-stage context when comparing results and seasons, and agree sampling frequency with your agronomist around useful decisions.

Josh / Cherry post-harvest — draft script

Post-harvest decisions should reflect how the block has performed this season. Your pack-out and fruit-quality results, crop load and applications are useful alongside the test results.

We’ve put together a checklist to work through with your agronomist. It covers which Nutrient findings need action, where a paired-leaf DSA could help with the next prescription and how to check whether that recommendation needs changing.

Repeat DSAs at relevant crop stages can build a seasonal record to help refine next season’s timing and prescriptions. The condition of the canopy, irrigation and roots also matters when deciding what can usefully be addressed after picking.

Download the checklist below. If you’d like to discuss your results or arrange testing, request a chat with me. You can also register interest in a post-harvest webinar and suggest questions you’d like covered. Dates and presenters are still being confirmed.

Broadacre / Soil testing

Soil testing checklist

The value of a soil test is in the recommendation that follows it. Use these points with your adviser to turn the raw readings into Nutrient requirements, decide what is worth correcting and develop a prescription that suits your soil and budget.

01

Turn the readings into kg/ha requirements

  • Work with an adviser who can interpret the raw readings into Nutrient requirements in kilograms per hectare to improve soil balance. See page 3 for the standard-test comparison and Hybrid-Ag’s approach.
  • Have the adviser explain which deficits or excesses warrant action and where a blanket application would supply Nutrients the soil does not need.
  • Check the basis of the kg/ha figures, including sampling depth, soil mass and the laboratory method. Separate the interpreted soil requirement from the eventual fertiliser rate.
02

Check the analysis is sufficient

  • Check that the analysis covers the relevant major and Trace Elements, alongside pH, salinity and other soil properties needed to interpret the results. Agree any gaps before paying for another test.
  • Review Nutrient results alongside constraints such as compaction or drainage. An application may not resolve the underlying reason an area is underperforming.
  • Compare historical results on a consistent basis. Differences in sampling depth, analytical method or units can change the apparent trend.
03

Target the samples before you collect them

  • Use DataFarming NDVI imagery with yield records and paddock history to identify contrasting production areas. Check the pattern on the ground before choosing sample zones.
  • Sample contrasting zones separately where results could change the prescription. Mark locations with GPS coordinates, for example in Google Earth, to guide repeat sampling.
  • Agree depths and methods with your adviser and laboratory. A field pH indicator can screen for acidity down the profile; confirm suspected layers with separate-depth laboratory samples.

A plan for your paddock

04

Build a prescription around the requirement

  • Use the interpreted kg/ha requirements to develop a Hybrid-Ag prescription blend, accounting for existing Nutrient contributions, crop demand, application options and budget.
  • Check what the proposed blend and rate actually supply per hectare. Resolve any unnecessary additions or remaining gaps before settling the recommendation.
  • Prioritise the corrections if the full recommendation exceeds the budget. Agree what to address first and what follow-up testing would justify the next investment.
Converting a concentration into kg/ha requires an appropriate soil-depth and soil-mass basis, with bulk density and gravel considered where relevant. An extracted amount is not automatically all crop-available, and the result is not itself a fertiliser rate.

Review progress across seasons

Comparable repeat tests can help review soil corrections. Keep sampling area, depth, timing and method consistent; review changes alongside crop performance and management records to plan next season’s work.

The full soil requirement may need staging over several seasons; it is not automatically a one-season application rate.

The Hamper Program

Every December, a crate lands on the doorstep of the growers who back us — packed with produce from our own client farms, branded gear worth keeping, and a card the whole team signed. It is the signature give-back touchpoint of the year: genuine reciprocity with the people we work for, and a second order of business — it strengthens the suppliers who are also our customers. 2025 shipped 171 hampers and lifted the bar on every part of the process. This page is everything we know, everything we learnt, and exactly what happens for 2026.

Why hampers, and why they sit here

Not a generic supplier gift — a working piece of the community & partners pillar.

The hamper is a reciprocity tool. It fills the goodwill bar with the sales team and with every client who receives one — which makes the next conversation, the next order and the next content ask easier. It is also deliberately built from our own ecosystem: the wine, macadamias, olive oil, almonds, dried fruit and flour are bought from growers we work with, so one budget strengthens two relationships at once. Coordination runs across Marketing, Sales, Accounts, Admin and Management — in 2025 that cross-functional alignment was markedly better than the year before.

“Thanking Hybrid for a generous and thoughtful hamper — the whole family enjoyed it.” Unprompted client email, January 2026. Taravale also featured our 1 kg flour order in their own behind-the-scenes Instagram reel — their first large order.

The 2025 cycle — what shipped, what it taught us

Three tiers, 171 crates, ~$35K. A clear uplift in execution — and a short, honest list of what to fix.

What actually shipped — the three 2025 tiers

What worked

  • Crates + engraved acrylic lids + black palette — premium finish, stackable, no cellophane fiddle. Slats double as handles.
  • Client-name labels on every crate — killed the mix-ups and enforced conditions (dairy farmers get no almonds; wine clients never get their own wine back).
  • Production-line packing — instructions on the wall, a sample to copy. Paige + Monique + Chelsea ran it in about a week.
  • Premium branded items — the black beanie in every crate, engraved Yeti for the top tier. Lifted the whole experience.
  • Pia’s documentation — allocation list, conditions sheet, tier-based packing list, supplier context, live delivery checklist. This is why 2026 is repeatable.
  • Supplier collaboration — discounts and flexibility nearly everywhere (15% off Kaesler, 35% off Handpicked, freight covered on dried fruit, nine bonus flour bags from Col at Taravale for staff).

What didn’t

  • Calendar ownership was unclear — Amos pre-ordered stock calendars for VARD without flagging Marketing. 2026: Marketing owns calendars, full stop.
  • Allocations changed mid-flight — 2026 gets a firm cut-off date; a late case has to be genuinely strong.
  • Content capture didn’t happen — deliveries ran through December and the reciprocity bar with sales was still empty. 2026: expectations set on day one, built through individual asks.
  • Card-signing session never ran — ad-hoc notes instead. 2026: formal boardroom session, aligned with the team’s in-person review days.
  • Hats arrived dusty — a $1 lint roller fixes it. Now a pre-pack QC step.
  • Recipe book + territory map didn’t make the cut — both carry into 2026.

The sessions — the record behind every decision

Three recorded meetings carried the program from history-gathering to a closed loop. The notes below are distilled from the full transcripts.

Session 1 · Discovery

History & supplier baseline — with Katie Rowe

8 October 2025 · Nathan Katseli & Katie Rowe

  • Katie walked through the 2022–24 playbook: crates beat baskets and long boxes; the cellophane-and-ribbon year took far longer; the etched acrylic lid is the premium look.
  • ~25–30 hampers left over from 2024 — an allocation gap, not a budget one. Leftover chocolates had melted upstairs: no meltables, ever.
  • Ideal rhythm: orders in within 1–2 weeks of early October, packed before 1 November, delivered mid-Nov to mid-Dec.
  • Tiers simplified on the call from four-way confusion to $400 / $200 / $120; allocation by GP + relationship (Amos’s steer), Hamilton to own the list.
  • The Costco insight: when Katie shopped in July she bought whisky at half retail — same budget, earlier sourcing, visibly bigger hampers.
  • Beanie for everyone (caps are an unwinnable style debate); engraved Yeti reserved for the top tier; avoid knives (freight) and $15–20 oil spray bottles.
  • Calendars: same stock artwork for years, surplus left behind, reps forget to take them — the seed of the client-photo calendar built from Hamilton’s farm photography.
  • Personalisation beats value: Torrens Valley gets Bundaberg rum because that’s what he likes. Worth a preferences tab in the CRM.

Session 2 · Packing & logistics

Locking the on-the-ground plan

11 November 2025 · Sophia Ong (Pia), Nathan Katseli, Paige Cox, Katie Rowe

  • Katie now Melbourne-based — remote advisory only. Paige leads packing on-site with Monique as buddy; Chelsea in for a half/full day.
  • Crates ready Friday 21 Nov; packing complete by 28 Nov; ~170 crates is a one-week job once everything lands.
  • The decision of the session: a name label on every crate — customer + agronomist — so conditions hold even when reps grab and go.
  • Personalised cards: Amos polished the thank-you copy, back-to-back print, team writes messages around it, card travels inside the crate — signed before packing even starts.
  • Delivery tracking moved from a printed tick-sheet to a live Odoo Knowledge checklist, shareable by link, reviewed at sales meetings.
  • Pia’s supplier tracker: 13 line items, tracked to the day — the visibility that made the week possible.
  • Packing space defaults to the storeroom; confirm with Amos (warehouse works were underway) and borrow muscle from the warehouse crew for carrying.

Session 3 · Result & review

Closing the loop — and setting up 2026

27 January 2026 · Sophia Ong (Pia) & Nathan Katseli

  • Verdict: a clear uplift in execution quality and structure — better documentation, coordination and far less pressure than 2024.
  • Two tiers for 2026, locked on the call: a standard and an extra-special. Firmer with sales — lock allocations at the meeting or miss out.
  • Allocations fall out of Hamilton’s forecasting tiers — ask for the list months earlier, then simply confirm outliers with the team.
  • Planning and ordering both pulled forward a month — September, on the logic that finished-early hampers just sit ready.
  • Costco/Aldi sourcing meeting with Katie before lock-in; reassess Yeti’s range at the time (the cooler-as-crate idea stays parked on cost).
  • Foliar 3-pack confirmed as the cheap, high-novelty 2026 addition — we manufacture the product, we only buy bottles.
  • Recipe book: refresh Chris’s 2022 design, regenerate recipes with AI around what’s actually in the crate.
  • The reciprocity-bar frame (from Influence): fill it individually — Chelsea’s magazine subscription → her data → her hamper-handover video.

Hard rules — do not revisit

  • No knives — freight and safety.
  • No chocolate or meltables — 40°C delivery runs; the 2024 leftovers melted upstairs.
  • Never return a grower’s own produce — substitute an equivalent.
  • No individual oil spray bottles — $15–20 of glass nobody asked for.
  • No caps — the style debate is unwinnable. Parked indefinitely.

Quality control — before anything ships

  • Build one sample of each tier first — photograph it; if it looks short, add filler or minor ribbon.
  • Lint-roll / brush every branded item — the 2025 hats arrived dusty.
  • Pre-label every crate with client + agronomist name before packing.
  • Keep the contents palette black / grey coordinated — it’s why the crates read premium.

The early-sourcing play

Katie’s July Costco runs bought whisky at half retail and premium goods at deep discount. Starting in September buys back that advantage: the same ~$35K delivers visibly more per crate. Run the sourcing meeting with Katie before any order is locked — and call Maree at Aussie Wine Crates first, because her lead time sets the whole calendar.

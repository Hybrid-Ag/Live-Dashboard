The three ways in

A call for the leads who put their hand up, a booked chat for the rest of the Apple list, and a session for the Cherry list. Each ends the same way: with the exclusive offer.

1 · Warm Apple leads · Josh Richardson

Now

Josh calls straight away

Four Apple leads ticked a chat on the form: Iain MacLennan asked for one now; Phil Shepherd, Paul Mason and Dougal Munro said later this season. Josh phones all four within the day, and every new one as it lands until the campaign closes.

There is no harm in touching base early with the “later” leads: a short hello, what they’re growing, and a time booked for when it suits them. The Cherry leads who ticked the same boxes in July were called the same way.

For They raised their hand, so it is the shortest path to a conversation; both of July’s “yes” leads turned into commercial follow-up; Josh has the time. Against “Later” can mean later, so the call offers a time rather than pushes; Josh is new and needs Chelsea behind him for the technical questions.

The exclusive offer

The free DSA and the test credit are no longer loose follow-on tools. They are packaged as one offer, only for people who took the call or stayed to the end of a session. The email promises it, the close reveals it. That gives a reason to turn up, a reason to stay, and a reason to buy.

The headline · revealed at the close

Free spring DSA on one block

One paired young-leaf / old-leaf DSA on a block of their choice, on us, kit posted the same day, read back by Josh, Chelsea or Nate. The “test, don’t guess” message of both Playbooks made real; the read-back is where the program conversation starts.

First 15 across both lists. Lab and freight cost is small against the first order it opens.

How the email sells it

Promise it, don’t spell it out

Apple: “There’s an offer at the end of the chat that we only make to the people who take it.” Cherry: “Stay to the end — there’s something on us for everyone in the room.”

The out-of-ten score is taken before the offer is made, so every DSA claimed already has a benchmark against it. Revisit after the program; the before and after is the success story.

Deadline and follow-up

A date on both offers, said plainly, and a follow-up rhythm that keeps going until the grower buys or asks us to stop.

The deadline

  • Friday 10 October. Wording in the emails and on the calls: “We’re holding spots until Friday 10 October” / “Registrations close Friday 10 October”. A date, not a countdown.
  • It is real: bloom to fruit set is an eight-week window and the DSA has to land inside it. Say so if asked, and hold it.
  • The exclusive offer is promised in the email but never spelled out there; the 15-DSA cap is said once, at the close.

The rhythm

  • Email with the offer → Josh’s call or Andrew’s invite → SMS receipt on any booking or registration → second email with the session recording → second call at fruit set → and on, until they buy or opt out
  • Five touches over six weeks is the floor, not the ceiling; Cherry’s 61 Playbook-only leads got one email and went quiet
  • A referral ask on every read-back: “know another grower who’d want one? We’ll test their block too”
  • Every touch logged against the lead in the shared sheet so nobody is chased twice or missed

What this round is for

The offers above are good; the proof behind them is thin. This round runs to fix that. Every prospect who takes the DSA and the program becomes a block story with a number in front and a number behind, and those stories are what the next version of these campaigns is built on.

1 · RunJosh and Andrew work their lists with the two offers. Uptake recorded per lane: emails sent, bookings and registrations, calls done, DSAs claimed, programs quoted, orders.
2 · RecordThe out-of-ten score before, the DSA result, the program, and the score after. Photos of the block where the grower is happy to. Consent to be named asked on the read-back, not at the end.
3 · RetellEach success becomes a testimonial, a block-transformation post and a line in the next Playbook email. The next campaign leads with proof, and the offers can then carry a guarantee.

What this round deliberately leaves out is listed under refinements for future campaigns.

Refinements for future campaigns

Ideas discussed and parked. This round is already a step up on how leads have been handled before; these are the improvements to make once it has run and the numbers are in.

When the numbers justify it

Separate sessions for growers and advisers

Growers hear problems and what fixed them; advisers hear testing and how to use the numbers. There are 19 advisers across the two lists today. Once a list is big enough to fill two rooms, split them, and give Josh an adviser version of the chat.

When advisers are their own track

An adviser offer

DSA at partner rates for their growers, read-back included on the first few, with Jamie opening the Certified Partner conversation. One adviser converting is worth many grower leads.

When there is proof

A guarantee on the program

Run the program as written; if the follow-up DSA hasn’t moved the deficient nutrients into range, the second round of product is on us. Needs success stories and Amos and Tom on the terms.

When there is proof

A named, stacked program

Sell an outcome rather than a test: DSA, read-back, written program, first-order credit and the session recording under one name, each line priced.

Next lead magnet

Block Nutrition Scorecard

Six questions in, a scored one-pager back. The out-of-ten question does this job by phone for now; the online version follows the website rebuild.

Next version of these campaigns

Creative built on results

This round’s before-and-after block stories become the ads, the email openers and the first slide of the next session, with tighter targeting from what we learn about who booked.

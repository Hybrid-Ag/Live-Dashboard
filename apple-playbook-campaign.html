Follow-on tools for Josh and Andrew

In the kit, used at their discretion once the first offer has been taken. The first two secure the next piece of business; the third is how we measure it.

After the call or session

Free spring DSA on the block

One paired young-leaf / old-leaf DSA on the block they named, on us, kit posted the same day, read back by Josh, Chelsea or Nate. The “test, don’t guess” message of both Playbooks made real; the read-back is where the program conversation starts.

Capped at the first 15 growers or managers across both lists. Lab and freight cost is small against the first order it opens.

At the quote

Test credited to the first order

Pia’s idea, and the same lever as the Henty Testing Investment Match: the grower pays for a block test, and the full amount comes off their first order this season. Used where the free DSA has already been claimed or the grower wants more than one block tested.

To confirm before it is offered: which tests qualify · maximum credit · minimum order · how long the credit stays valid · one per business.

The benchmark

The out-of-ten question

Growers are cagey about pack-out and the team is hesitant to ask. So don’t ask for the number, ask for the score: “out of ten, how did that block pack out?” They answer with a score or, often, the real figure. Either way it is written down with the block and the date.

Revisit it after the program. The before and after is the success story, and the success story is the next campaign’s creative.

Deadline and follow-up

A date on both offers, said plainly, and a follow-up rhythm that keeps going until the grower buys or asks us to stop.

The deadline

  • Friday 10 October. Wording in the emails and on the calls: “We’re holding call spots until Friday 10 October” / “Registrations close Friday 10 October”. A date, not a countdown.
  • It is real: bloom to fruit set is an eight-week window and the DSA has to land inside it. Say so if asked, and hold it.
  • The 15-DSA cap is stated once, in the email, and not repeated.

The rhythm

  • Email with the offer → Josh’s call or Andrew’s invite → SMS receipt on any booking or registration → second email with the session recording → second call at fruit set → and on, until they buy or opt out
  • Five touches over six weeks is the floor, not the ceiling; Cherry’s 61 Playbook-only leads got one email and went quiet
  • A referral ask on every read-back: “know another grower who’d want one? We’ll test their block too”
  • Every touch logged against the lead in the shared sheet so nobody is chased twice or missed

What this round is for

The offers above are good; the proof behind them is thin. This round runs to fix that. Every prospect who takes the DSA and the program becomes a block story with a number in front and a number behind, and those stories are what the next version of these campaigns is built on.

1 · RunJosh and Andrew work their lists with the two offers. Uptake recorded per lane: emails sent, bookings and registrations, calls done, DSAs claimed, programs quoted, orders.
2 · RecordThe out-of-ten score before, the DSA result, the program, and the score after. Photos of the block where the grower is happy to. Consent to be named asked on the read-back, not at the end.
3 · RetellEach success becomes a testimonial, a block-transformation post and a line in the next Playbook email. The next campaign leads with proof, and the offers can then carry a guarantee.

Parked until there is proof: a conditional guarantee on the program, and selling a named, stacked “Spring Pack-Out Program”. Both need success stories under them first. The Block Nutrition Scorecard as a lead magnet is folded into the out-of-ten question above.

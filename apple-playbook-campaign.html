Follow-up offer options

What we send the 37 Playbook-only leads and the 3 maybes after the Playbook. Pia’s two ideas first, as written in Notion on 23 September, then four alternatives to run over with management. Nothing here is decided.

Pia · idea 1

Apple Playbook follow-up drip

Audience: leads who chose “Maybe, later this season” or “I’ll reach out if I need help”.

After the Playbook, ask the grower which topic matters most on their orchard: testing and understanding results · tree demand at growth stages (nutrition by crop stage) · water quality and nutrient timing / building the block program · fruit quality and pack-out / products and application options.

The pick triggers a follow-up email, or a call where it fits, with the matching Playbook pages, a video, or product and service information. Anyone who asks for pricing or contact goes to sales. No pick = general nurture list until they unsubscribe.

Pia · idea 2

Test fee credited on the first order

“Pay for an eligible test on an apple block. Place your first order with Hybrid-Ag within the agreed period and we credit the full test fee to that order.”

Paying up front means they intend to use the test; the credit gives them a reason to buy. If no order follows, the test stays a paid service. Promoted through the Playbook follow-up emails; growers reply or submit a request and we confirm test, cost and conditions before booking.

To confirm: which tests qualify · maximum credit · minimum order value · products covered · how long the credit stays valid · one offer per business.

Alternative A

15-minute block review with Chelsea — limited spring slots

A booked call, not “reply if you want”. Ten slots in the first two weeks of October, first come. Cherry showed the chat option pulls low volume but real quality (both yes-leads turned into commercial follow-up); this puts the same door in front of the 37 who didn’t take it on the form. Costs Chelsea’s time only.

Alternative B

Spring DSA at cost, interpreted

One paired young-leaf / old-leaf DSA on a block of their choice at our cost price, with Chelsea or Nate reading it back to them. It is the Playbook’s own “test, don’t guess” message made real, and the read-back is the sales conversation. Cheaper for the grower than idea 2 and no order condition to police; the sample and the conversation are the qualifier.

Alternative C

Andrew Smith spring Q&A, one hour online

One session in early October for all Apple and Cherry Playbook leads: what to check from bloom to fruit set, questions taken live. Scales to the whole list at once, gives Andrew’s credibility to people who only saw a 29-second video, and the registration itself is a second qualifier. Record it and it becomes the nurture email for everyone who doesn’t show.

Alternative D

Orchard visit when we’re in your district

Thirty-five of the 41 sit in two clusters, Goulburn/Yarra and the Adelaide Hills. Email each cluster a week we’ll be through and offer a free half-hour walk of one block. Turns the lead list into a route for Chelsea and Nate rather than 37 individual chases, and a visit is the strongest converter we have.

Pia’s drip (idea 1) can sit under any of the four; it is the delivery mechanism, the others are the offer.

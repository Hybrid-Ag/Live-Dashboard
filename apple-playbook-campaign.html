Tools in the arsenal

Step one: approve the tools. Six follow-up plays the team could use on these leads — Pia’s test credit, as written in Notion on 23 September, then five more — each with the case for and against. Tick the ones that are in; the route comes next.

Tool 1 · Pia

Test fee credited on the first order

“Pay for an eligible test on an apple block. Place your first order with Hybrid-Ag within the agreed period and we credit the full test fee to that order.”

Paying up front means they intend to use the test; the credit gives them a reason to buy. If no order follows, the test stays a paid service. Promoted through the Playbook follow-up emails; growers reply or submit a request and we confirm test, cost and conditions before booking.

To confirm: which tests qualify · maximum credit · minimum order value · products covered · how long the credit stays valid · one offer per business.

Pros Self-qualifying — only growers who mean it pay for a test; the credit ties the test to a first order; no free giveaway to police; fits the “test, don’t guess” message.

Cons Asks a cold lead to pay before we have spoken to them, so take-up will be low from a $2 lead; six conditions to agree and administer in Odoo; a grower who tests and buys nothing feels sold to; competes with the “free Nutrient Audit” the ad copy already promises.

Tool 2

15-minute block review with Josh or Chelsea

A booked call, not “reply if you want”. Josh takes the calls as the default — he is new to the team, agronomy-trained and in the office far more than Chelsea — with Chelsea for anyone who asks for her or where a block needs her. Offer slots through the first two weeks of October. Cherry showed the chat option pulls low volume but real quality (both yes-leads turned into commercial follow-up); this puts the same door in front of the 37 who didn’t take it on the form.

Pros No hard cost and Josh has the time now; a booked slot is a real commitment; every call is a qualification and a sales conversation in one; Josh learns the apple accounts from day one; can be live in a day with a booking link, and Josh can phone the ones who don’t book.

Cons Josh is new, so the first calls need a script and Chelsea behind him for the technical questions; a 15-minute call rarely closes anything on its own, it needs a next step behind it; take-up from people who ticked “I’ll reach out” may be a handful.

Tool 3

Free spring DSA, interpreted

One paired young-leaf / old-leaf DSA on a block of their choice, on us, with Josh, Chelsea or Nate reading it back to them. It is the Playbook’s own “test, don’t guess” message made real, and the read-back is the sales conversation. The lab cost of one DSA is a fraction of the first order it opens, and nothing to police: the sample and the conversation are the qualifier.

Pros Free removes the last objection; puts our core product (DSA + interpretation) in their hands at the moment it matters; the read-back is a natural program conversation; the result stays in our system for follow-up and the V2 app; the cost per test is small against a single first order.

Cons Real lab and freight cost per test, so cap it (say the first 15 growers or managers); sampling has to be done properly (kits, instructions, timing) so there is coordination; spring is the busiest time for the agronomists to interpret; a bad or late result reflects on us.

Tool 4

Andrew Smith spring Q&A, one hour online

One session in early October for all Apple and Cherry Playbook leads: what to check from bloom to fruit set, questions taken live. Scales to the whole list at once, gives Andrew’s credibility to people who only saw a 29-second video, and the registration itself is a second qualifier. Record it and it becomes the nurture email for everyone who doesn’t show.

Pros One hour of Andrew reaches all 120-odd Apple and Cherry leads at once; registrations show who is engaged; the recording becomes reusable content and a nurture email; timely for bloom to fruit set.

Cons Webinar attendance from growers is typically 10–20% of registrations; needs Andrew’s buy-in and a date inside two weeks; setup, invite and hosting fall on marketing; no direct commercial ask unless we build one into the close.

Tool 5

Orchard visit when we’re in your district

Thirty-five of the 41 sit in two clusters, Goulburn/Yarra and the Adelaide Hills. Email each cluster a week we’ll be through and offer a free half-hour walk of one block. Turns the lead list into a route for Chelsea and Nate rather than 37 individual chases, and a visit is the strongest converter we have.

Pros A visit is how we actually win orchards (Vince Blefari went from Cherry lead to program after two visits); batching by district makes it efficient; a named week creates urgency; the offer costs nothing until someone says yes.

Cons Depends on Chelsea and Nate being in those districts in the window; a half-hour walk can stretch to a morning; only the growers and managers (24 of 41) are worth a visit; needs a simple booking mechanism so we are not trading emails.

Tool 6

Extend the Testing Investment Match

Pia rebuilt the Testing Investment Match landing page for Henty this week: the grower’s testing spend matched as product credit. Point Apple leads at the same page. It is Tool 1 with the mechanics, wording and landing page already built.

Pros Nothing new to build or explain; one offer across broadacre and horticulture; the credit is tied to product, so redeeming it is an order; already approved for Henty.

Cons The match terms were set for broadacre testing and may not suit a single apple block; still asks for money up front; two credit offers (Tools 1 and 6) cannot both run, pick one.

Tools 1 and 6 are the same lever in two forms; run one. Whatever is chosen goes out through the Playbook email in Odoo.

Suggested route: a ladder, not a single offer

Step two: agree the route. With the approved tools in hand, this is the suggested way to run them: every lead gets the same first two rungs, then the team chooses the tool that fits the grower. Each rung is a small yes that earns the next. How hard Josh pushes leads up the ladder by phone is the dial management sets.

    Odoo #87 goes to every lead within 24 hours. Two versions: one for “maybe, later this season”, one for “I’ll reach out”.

    The audit read-back becomes a program; the program becomes a quote. Any test-credit or match offer is applied here, on the first order.

    Owner: Chelsea + Jamie · the ask: run the program this season

Where the approved tools sit. The cards above are the arsenal, not competing plans. The block call (tool 2) is the second rung; the Q&A (tool 4) and district visits (tool 5) are second-rung alternatives for people who won’t take a call; the free DSA (tool 3) is a lighter third rung than the full audit; the test credit or investment match (tools 1 and 6) sharpen the fourth. Anyone who stalls stays on the Playbook email list until spring gives them a reason.

The dial. With Josh on the phones the ladder is worked; without him it is offered. Same rungs either way, different speed.

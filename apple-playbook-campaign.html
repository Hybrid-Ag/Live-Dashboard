The three offers in detail

Three tools for Josh and Andrew, in the order they come out. The first is the standard next step: Josh plays it on the call, Andrew makes it to everyone who stays to the end of the session. The second and third are held back, and used only when the situation calls for them.

1 · The standard offer

Standard

Data clean-up and trends

Most growers have years of results scattered across labs, inboxes and folders. We request their data from every lab they have used, take whatever paperwork they hold, clean it up and pull it into trends, so they and we have a clearer picture of which way the orchard is heading and which inputs or actions would give the best result from here.

Josh plays it on the call when the conversation earns it; Andrew makes it in the last minutes of the session. Once the grower has agreed the scope and given the go-ahead, Rey gathers the data from the labs and, with Nathan, pulls it into trends on a quick dashboard for Josh or Andrew to use in every conversation with that grower. It costs us time, not product, and it earns the second conversation.

For Useful to the grower whether or not they buy; hard for anyone else to match; gives Josh and Andrew the full history before they recommend anything; the read-back is a natural second meeting. Against Time for Rey and Nathan to gather and build each set; depends on labs releasing results and growers finding their paperwork; needs the grower’s written OK to request their data.

2 · Held back · for objections

Discretion

Free soil test or DSA

One soil test or one paired DSA on a block of their choice, on us, so they can start with us and see what our reports give them.

Not part of the standard offer. Josh and Andrew use it only when a lead raises an objection, or they are struggling to land an appointment or a next step on knowledge and good sales craft alone. It is never in the email and never offered first.

For Removes the “I don’t know what I’d get” objection; the report does the selling; small cost against a first order. Against Given away too easily it becomes the expectation and cheapens the testing; every use should have a reason Josh or Andrew can name.

3 · Last resort · large prospects only

Discretion

We’ll match your testing investment

Whatever they invest in testing with us, we match. The mechanics are already built on the Henty Testing Investment Match page.

Reserved for genuinely large potential customers who are on the fence about going further. It is the last thing offered, not the first, and it is not for every lead: if the account is not big enough to justify it, it stays in the pocket. If in doubt, check with Jamie before offering.

For A serious commitment to a serious prospect; tips a large account that is close; tied to their own spend. Against The most expensive tool in the kit; offered widely it costs real margin and sets a precedent, which is why it is reserved.

How the Cherry invitation sells it

Promise it, don’t spell it out. “Stay to the end — there’s something on us for everyone in the room.” On Josh’s calls there is nothing to promise in advance; the offer comes when the conversation has earned it. The Apple campaign as built in Meta. The Cherry campaign it was modelled on (1–7 July, 78 leads, $2.39 a lead) and its full write-up sit on the Paid Lead Generation page in Notion.

Campaign

  • Name: Apple Playbook – Leads – September 2026
  • Objective: leads via Meta instant form
  • Budget: $200 lifetime, about $28/day over 7 days; low performers switched off as data comes in
  • Live 22 September 8am to 29 September 8am
  • One ad set, three creatives; low performers switched off as data comes in
  • Changes from Cherry: apple regions instead of cherry, spring and whole-season messaging, “where are the orchards you work with” region question, a friendlier “I’ll reach out” option, Apple submitters excluded for 90 days while Cherry leads stay eligible

Audience

  • Apple regions: Batlow, Bilpin, Orange (NSW) · Stanthorpe (QLD) · Berri, Lenswood, Mount Gambier (SA) · Bacchus Marsh, Coldstream, Harcourt, Red Hill, Shepparton, Stanley, Warragul (VIC), each with a 12–28 mile radius
  • Excludes Tasmania and Western Australia
  • Age 25–65+, all genders
  • Interest: Horticulture · Job title: Farm Owner Operator (audience signal, not a hard boundary)
  • Excludes anyone who has already submitted the Apple form (90 days); Cherry Playbook leads stay eligible

Instant form

  • Full name, email, phone
  • Your role: grower or owner · farm, orchard or block manager · agronomist or adviser · packhouse or marketing · other
  • Where are the apple orchards you mainly work with located? VIC · NSW · QLD · SA · other
  • Would you like a quick chat with Chelsea Hall about your orchard? Yes · maybe, later this season · I’ll reach out if I need help

After submit

  • “Download the Playbook” opens the digital Playbook straight away: Apple Nutrition Playbook (Odoo Documents)
  • As built, “Yes” answers were to go to Chelsea. As run: every lead is loaded into Enquiries on the dashboard and assigned to Josh Richardson, who calls them all
  • First follow-up email drafted (Odoo #87), offer and nurture sequence to be chosen — see above

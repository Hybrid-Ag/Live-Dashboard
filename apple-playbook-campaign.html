The three offers in detail

Three tools for Josh and Andrew, in the order they come out. The first is the standard next step: Josh plays it on the call, Andrew makes it to everyone who stays to the end of the session. The second and third are held back, and used only when the situation calls for them.

1 · The standard offer

Standard

Data clean-up and trends

Most growers have years of results scattered across labs, inboxes and folders. We request their data from every lab they have used, take whatever paperwork they hold, clean it up and pull it into trends, so they and we have a clearer picture of which way the orchard is heading and which inputs or actions would give the best result from here.

Josh plays it on the call when the conversation earns it; Andrew makes it in the last minutes of the session. It costs us time, not product, and it earns the second conversation.

For Useful to the grower whether or not they buy; hard for anyone else to match; gives Josh and Andrew the full history before they recommend anything; the read-back is a natural second meeting. Against Time for agronomic support to gather and clean each set; depends on labs releasing results and growers finding their paperwork; needs the grower’s written OK to request their data.

2 · Held back · for objections

Discretion

Free soil test or DSA

One soil test or one paired DSA on a block of their choice, on us, so they can start with us and see what our reports give them.

Not part of the standard offer. Josh and Andrew use it only when a lead raises an objection, or they are struggling to land an appointment or a next step on knowledge and good sales craft alone. It is never in the email and never offered first.

For Removes the “I don’t know what I’d get” objection; the report does the selling; small cost against a first order. Against Given away too easily it becomes the expectation and cheapens the testing; every use should have a reason Josh or Andrew can name.

3 · Last resort · large prospects only

Discretion

We’ll match your testing investment

Whatever they invest in testing with us, we match. The mechanics are already built on the Henty Testing Investment Match page.

Reserved for genuinely large potential customers who are on the fence about going further. It is the last thing offered, not the first, and it is not for every lead: if the account is not big enough to justify it, it stays in the pocket. If in doubt, check with Jamie before offering.

For A serious commitment to a serious prospect; tips a large account that is close; tied to their own spend. Against The most expensive tool in the kit; offered widely it costs real margin and sets a precedent, which is why it is reserved.

How the Cherry invitation sells it

Promise it, don’t spell it out. “Stay to the end — there’s something on us for everyone in the room.” On Josh’s calls there is nothing to promise in advance; the offer comes when the conversation has earned it. Either way the out-of-ten score is taken first, so every data clean-up starts with a benchmark against it. Revisit after the program; the before and after is the success story.

Deadline and follow-up

A date on both offers, said plainly, and a follow-up rhythm that keeps going until the grower buys or asks us to stop.

The deadline

  • Friday 10 October. Wording on the calls and in the emails: “I’m holding meeting spots until Friday 10 October” / “Registrations close Friday 10 October”. A date, not a countdown.
  • It is real: bloom to fruit set is an eight-week window and the picture has to be clear while it can still change this season’s decisions. Say so if asked, and hold it.
  • The Cherry invitation promises the offer but never spells it out. The two held-back tools are never mentioned in writing.

The rhythm

  • Apple: Josh’s call → a second try → the missed-call email with a pick-a-time link → SMS receipt on any booking → the next meeting → a call at fruit set → and on, until they buy or opt out
  • Cherry: the invitation → a reminder → the session → the recording to everyone who registered → a call from Andrew or Josh to those who took the offer → and on
  • Five touches over six weeks is the floor, not the ceiling; Cherry’s 61 Playbook-only leads got one email and went quiet
  • A referral ask on every read-back: “know another grower who’d want one? We’ll test their block too”
  • Every touch logged against the lead in the shared sheet so nobody is chased twice or missed

What this round is for

The offers above are good; the proof behind them is thin. This round runs to fix that. Every prospect who takes the DSA and the program becomes a block story with a number in front and a number behind, and those stories are what the next version of these campaigns is built on.

1 · RunJosh calls his list and notes what works and what doesn’t; Andrew runs his session. Uptake recorded per play: calls made and reached, cards played and which landed, registrations and attendance, data clean-ups taken, held-back tools used and why, programs quoted, orders.
2 · RecordThe out-of-ten score before, the cleaned-up data and its trends, the program, and the score after. Photos of the block where the grower is happy to. Consent to be named asked on the read-back, not at the end.
3 · RetellEach success becomes a testimonial, a block-transformation post and a line in the next Playbook email. The next campaign leads with proof, and the offers can then carry a guarantee.

Refinements for future campaigns

Ideas discussed and parked. This round is already a step up on how leads have been handled before; these are the improvements to make once it has run and the numbers are in.

When the numbers justify it

Separate sessions for growers and advisers

Growers hear problems and what fixed them; advisers hear testing and how to use the numbers. There are 19 advisers across the two lists today. Once a list is big enough to fill two rooms, split them, and give Josh an adviser version of the chat.

When advisers are their own track

An adviser offer

DSA at partner rates for their growers, read-back included on the first few, with Jamie opening the Certified Partner conversation. One adviser converting is worth many grower leads.

When there is proof

A guarantee on the program

Run the program as written; if the follow-up DSA hasn’t moved the deficient nutrients into range, the second round of product is on us. Needs success stories and Amos and Tom on the terms.

When there is proof

A named, stacked program

Sell an outcome rather than a test: data clean-up, testing, read-back, written program and the session recording under one name, each line priced.

Next lead magnet

Block Nutrition Scorecard

Six questions in, a scored one-pager back. The out-of-ten question does this job by phone for now; the online version follows the website rebuild.

Next version of these campaigns

Creative built on results

This round’s before-and-after block stories become the ads, the email openers and the first slide of the next session, with tighter targeting from what we learn about who booked.

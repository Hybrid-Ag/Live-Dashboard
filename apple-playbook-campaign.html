The crop now and the next season

Start with the variety, crop stage and what the grower wants to achieve.

  1. 01

    What can I still influence before harvest?

    Andrew’s guide starts with the variety, stage and time to harvest; the Playbook supplies further context.
    Testing: leaf or sap analysis describes the sampled material. Read it alongside crop load, fruit growth and canopy observations; it does not measure fruit cell number or composition.

  2. 02

    What could stop me getting the fruit quality I want?

    Andrew’s guide works back from the fruit quality and market requirement the grower wants.
    Testing: consider fruit measurements, crop observations and relevant soil or plant results. Previous pack-out records add context; they do not prove what caused this season’s result.

  3. 03

    What should I plan for after harvest?

    Andrew’s guide and The Hidden Season connect the current crop with developing buds and the period after picking.
    Testing: agree which soil or plant information would help plan the next step and when to collect it. Leaf or sap results do not directly measure the tree’s carbohydrate reserves.

Nutrition and fruit quality

Establish the crop’s needs before choosing an application.

  1. 04

    How do I know whether Calcium is reaching the fruit?

    Andrew’s guide raises Calcium delivery as the fruit develops; the Playbook and shuck-fall post provide background for his review.
    Testing: a leaf or paired-leaf sap result does not directly measure Calcium in the cherry. Andrew confirms what fruit measurements and orchard context would be needed.

  2. 05

    How do Calcium and Potassium requirements change as the fruit develops?

    Andrew’s guide considers each Nutrient in the context of crop stage and measured status. It does not establish a universal takeover or ratio.
    Testing: compare results only where methods and sampling are comparable. Andrew confirms how each result relates to the question being asked.

  3. 06

    When would I use soil, foliar or fertigation applications?

    The Playbook discusses application methods; Andrew confirms what is suitable for the block and stage.
    Testing: soil and plant results help establish the need. Where a Nutrient need is supported, discuss the prescription blend and suitable application method; sap is not a direct measure of delivery into fruit.

  4. 07

    Rain near harvest: what can nutrition do about cracking, and what are its limits?

    A question from the original bank for Andrew to assess against the expected crop stage and conditions.
    Testing: a Calcium result alone cannot establish cracking risk or promise protection. Andrew confirms which evidence and limitations belong in the answer.

Making sense of the results

Use the result alongside what is happening in the orchard.

  1. 08

    What can one leaf test tell me?

    The Playbook compares soil, leaf and sap testing; Andrew’s guide asks what information would improve a decision.
    Testing: explain what was sampled, what the method reports and what it cannot establish. Keep dates, leaf ages and crop stage with the result.

  2. 09

    How do I use soil results alongside what I’m seeing in the crop?

    Andrew’s guide considers roots, water, canopy and crop load before deciding on a Nutrient response.
    Testing: a soil test and a plant sample describe different material. Read them with observations from the block rather than treating either as a direct measure of root uptake.

  3. 10

    How do I check whether the canopy can support the crop?

    Andrew’s guide asks about leaf area, shoot growth, crop demand and canopy condition.
    Testing: plant analysis can add Nutrient information but does not directly measure photosynthesis. Andrew confirms which observations and results are relevant; no fixed group of Elements answers the question.

Campaign

  • Name: Apple Playbook – Leads – September 2026
  • Objective: leads via Meta instant form
  • Budget: $200 lifetime, about $28/day over 7 days; low performers switched off as data comes in
  • Live 22 September 8am to 29 September 8am
  • One ad set, three creatives; low performers switched off as data comes in
  • Changes from Cherry: apple regions instead of cherry, spring and whole-season messaging, “where are the orchards you work with” region question, a friendlier “I’ll reach out” option, Apple submitters excluded for 90 days while Cherry leads stay eligible

Audience

  • Apple regions: Batlow, Bilpin, Orange (NSW) · Stanthorpe (QLD) · Berri, Lenswood, Mount Gambier (SA) · Bacchus Marsh, Coldstream, Harcourt, Red Hill, Shepparton, Stanley, Warragul (VIC), each with a 12–28 mile radius
  • Excludes Tasmania and Western Australia
  • Age 25–65+, all genders
  • Interest: Horticulture · Job title: Farm Owner Operator (audience signal, not a hard boundary)
  • Excludes anyone who has already submitted the Apple form (90 days); Cherry Playbook leads stay eligible

Instant form

  • Full name, email, phone
  • Your role: grower or owner · farm, orchard or block manager · agronomist or adviser · packhouse or marketing · other
  • Where are the apple orchards you mainly work with located? VIC · NSW · QLD · SA · other
  • Would you like a quick chat with Chelsea Hall about your orchard? Yes · maybe, later this season · I’ll reach out if I need help

After submit

  • “Download the Playbook” opens the digital Playbook straight away: Apple Nutrition Playbook (Odoo Documents)
  • As built, “Yes” answers were to go to Chelsea. As run: every lead is loaded into Enquiries on the dashboard and assigned to Josh Richardson, who calls them all
  • Follow-up is by phone: Josh calls every lead. The missed-call email draft sits in the flowchart
